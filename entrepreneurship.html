OUR COMMERCIAL DIRECTION

MELANIFY explores a B2B ingredient-supplier model: upcycling PET-derived carbon into allomelanin for future cosmetic formulations. Our material remains at laboratory scale. The market figures, costs and product specifications below are planning estimates and targets.

01From plastic waste to a valuable pigment

Every year, Thailand produces approximately 1.9 million tons of plastic. However, only around a quarter of the country’s plastic production is recycled and reused. The number of PET bottles collected annually alone is well within the hundreds of thousands of tons, with a recycling rate of about 4.5% [1,2]. Also Thailand, a country with a 35.5 million-visitor tourism economy [3], depends on coral reefs that are currently being destroyed by UV-filter chemicals in the water from tourists. This exact reason is why Thailand has banned four reef-toxic sunscreen filters in its marine national parks in 2021 [4].

Seeing these unresolved problems, Melanify decided to take initiative and intends to release a remedy to both issues. Our solution converts PET-derived carbon into allomelanin, a melanin class that has historically shown broad-spectrum UV absorption and antioxidant activity [9][10]. By transforming plastic waste into a valuable pigment, Melanify aims to create a solution that addresses PET pollution all the while supporting the development of more environmentally responsible sun-care products.

Melanify’s business model aims to generate income from three sources: fees for accepting PET waste, sales of the resulting allomelanin pigment, and verified plastic credits for diverting waste from the environment.

02The opportunity in Thailand

Thailand is among the world’s largest plastic polluters. National figures put plastic waste at around 1.9 million tons per year with only around 25% of it being recycled. PET plastic bottles are collected at around 600,000 tons per year. Still, the effective bottle-to-bottle recycling rate has been reported to be as low as 4.5%, numbers far below Japan (83%) or Germany (94%) [1][2]. PET plastics that aren’t recycled end up in landfills, being incinerated, or leaking into waterways and the sea.

In 2024, Thailand hosted 35.5 million international tourists [3], with the main attractions of the country being marine national parks. An example of this is Nopparat Thara in the Phi Phi Island generating over 500 million THB in revenue within a single season [3]. Oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-MBC and butylparaben (common chemical UV filters) have been documented to accelerate the bleaching of corals and the disruption of larval development. This is why Thailand’s Department of National Parks banned them in marine parks since August of 2021. Fines for breaking the rules have been recorded to be up to 100,000 THB [4].

Reef-safe formulators today are boxed into inorganic actives (ZnO, TiO2) that deliver protection while still enacting a constant ‘white-cast’ problem and no antioxidant benefit. There is a gap for a reef-safe, biodegradable, and antioxidant ingredient that improves formulation performance. That gap is where Melanify comes in.

Melanify’s business model combines three potential revenue streams: waste-management fees, pigment sales, and environmental credits. First, Melanify aims to receive tipping fees or extended producer responsibility (EPR) for accepting and processing post-consumer PET. This helps to offset the raw-material costs that are used. Second, we plan to sell the allomelanin pigment that is produced, by the kilogram, to sunscreen and cosmetics formulators. Third, it is possible to earn additional income through verified plastic credits for waste collection or recycling.

What the literature suggests

Melanin is nature’s own photoprotectant: melanogenesis in human skin absorbs an estimated 50-75% of incident UV [9]. Many published articles on bacterial and fungal melanin-blended creams report that SPF enhancements range from around 1.95 to 26 depending on how much is used. Melanin-blended cream reached an in-vitro SPF of around 2.5 at 5% pigment and a UVA/UVB > 0.81 [9]. Allomelanin specifically (derived from 1,8-dihydroxynaphthalene) has shown photoprotective and antioxidant performance exceeding that of polydopamine. The reason allomelanin works so well is mainly due to its two protection mechanisms (light extinction and radical scavenging).

These published SPF and antioxidant values describe other melanin materials, including 1,8-DHN allomelanin. They are not measurements of MELANIFY’s catechol-derived material. Our own efficacy and safety have not yet been established.

08SWOT analysis

Strengths

  • Potential negative-cost PET feedstock, if processing fees are secured.
  • Multifunctional target: UV booster, antioxidant and colorant.
  • A design goal of biodegradable, non-nano material with verified environmental compatibility.
  • A waste-to-value narrative and a platform with multiple downstream possibilities.

Weaknesses

  • Low current laboratory yield and unproven scale-up.
  • No in-house SPF or efficacy data for our material.
  • Dark colour constrains formulation and inclusion levels.
  • A five-step, two-organism process adds operational complexity.
  • No GMP production, Certificate of Analysis or toxicology dossier yet.

Opportunities

  • Interest in alternatives following marine-park filter restrictions.
  • Tinted mineral-sunscreen formulations and natural pigments.
  • Potential EPR / verified plastic-credit income.
  • ASEAN and global clean-beauty markets.
  • Licensing bioconversion technology to established manufacturers.

Threats

  • Low-cost approved minerals are already established.
  • Regulatory classification and claim-substantiation requirements.
  • Consumer concerns about a plastic-derived skin-care ingredient.
  • Large ingredient makers could develop competing processes.
  • Fermentation costs may remain above target.

13What still needs to be demonstrated

We state these plainly, because a business case that hides them is not credible.

  • Performance of our material is untested. Literature SPF and antioxidant figures are not measurements of MELANIFY allomelanin. ISO 24443 / 24444 validation is future work.
  • Yields are low. The cost model projects a scaled process; current titres are below the modelled 5–15 g/L, and scale-up risk is real.
  • No regulatory dossier yet. Cosmetic-ingredient notification, toxicology testing and a Certificate of Analysis remain to be completed.
  • Market assumptions are estimates. Reef-safe share, inclusion rate and price are model assumptions rather than measured market data.
  • No signed customer yet. Named brands are illustrative targets, not committed partners.

These are our next milestones. Phase 1 funding and testing are intended to resolve them before a commercial launch.
